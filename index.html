      How to Write a Great LinkedIn Headline

      Recruiters spend only a few seconds scanning search results before deciding whose profile to click. A strong LinkedIn bio headline answers three fast questions: what job you do, what tools or domain you know, and what results you bring.

      01

      Put your exact target job title first

      LinkedIn truncates headlines around 60 to 80 characters in comments, mobile feeds, and Recruiter search previews. Lead with the exact title hiring managers type into the search bar—like "Senior Data Analyst" or "Account Executive"—instead of vague phrases like "Passionate Problem Solver."

      02

      Add 2 to 4 searchable hard skills

      Think of your headline like an ATS-friendly resume header. Include the specific platforms, certifications, or specialties recruiters filter for (for example, "SQL, Tableau, Python" or "ICU, BSN, RN"). Skip generic soft skills that nobody searches for.

      03

      Back it up with one concrete result

      If you have room left inside the 220-character limit, add a real number or clear outcome—like "Cut cloud spend by 32%" or "Helping Series A teams hire engineers." Plain proof beats hype every time.

      04

      Keep formatting clean and scannable

      Break up your headline with spaces around pipes (|), bullets (•), or slashes (/). Avoid walls of emojis or ALL CAPS, which hurt readability on mobile screens and can break keyword matching in search.

      Frequently Asked Questions

      Quick answers to the most common questions about writing and updating your LinkedIn profile headline.

      What is the LinkedIn headline character limit?

      LinkedIn allows up to 220 characters in your profile headline, including spaces and punctuation. However, only the first 60 to 80 characters appear next to your name in comments, connection requests, and mobile search results, so always put your core job title first.

      Should I put "Seeking new opportunities" in my LinkedIn headline?

      Usually no. Recruiters rarely search for the phrase "seeking new opportunities" in LinkedIn Recruiter—they search for job titles and hard skills like "Senior Financial Analyst" or "React Developer." Use your 220 characters for your target role and top skills, and turn on LinkedIn's built-in Open to Work setting instead.

      How does LinkedIn's search algorithm read profile headlines?

      Your headline is one of the highest-weighted fields in LinkedIn search. When a hiring manager searches for a role plus two or three skills, profiles with exact matches in the headline tend to appear higher than profiles that only mention those terms deep in the Experience section.

      Is this LinkedIn headline generator free and private?

      Yes. This tool is 100% free with no account required and runs entirely in your browser. Your job title, skills, and saved headlines are never sent to a server—saved favorites stay in your own browser's local storage.

      Which separator works best in a LinkedIn headline: pipe, bullet, or slash?

      Pipes (|) and bullets (•) are the easiest to scan quickly and keep separate phrases visually distinct for both human recruiters and search parsers. Leave a single space on both sides of each separator so LinkedIn indexes every keyword cleanly.
